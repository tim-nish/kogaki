Issue: https://github.com/tim-nish/kogaki/issues/163. On conflict, the issue
wins — and where the owner ruling at
https://github.com/tim-nish/kogaki/issues/168 governs this issue, the ruling
wins over both.

As the Kogaki repository owner, I want the composer's input bounded to the
tag-scoped served Gloss shard when it composes GroupClaims and SubGroupClaims,
so that the co-tag step stops re-reading every group's full material once per
group it appears in and the wall clock between naming a tag and seeing
something stops growing with the placement count.

## Context — the measurement

Dogfood run 2026-08-07, tag `architecture`: 70 Lessons, 11 co-tag groups,
131 placements. Survey record written 11:14; all 11 Full Reports written
11:33 — **~19 minutes between naming the tag and anything appearing**, against
under five in previous runs. The runtime is not the cost: re-running `cotags`
read-only on the same survey record renders 11 groups instantly, and the 11
reports landed within one minute of each other once composition existed. The
cost is composition, and it is quadratic-ish in the wrong quantity: each
Lesson is re-read once per group it appears in, so 70 Lessons cost 131 reads.

## Scope — one of the issue's three levers, and why the other two are absent

**Lever 1 (enforce §8.1's never-default so the judge pass leaves the default
path) is OVERRULED and is not in this story.** The owner ruling at kogaki#168
rules SubGroups REQUIRED on the screen and in the Full Reports and says so of
this issue by name: *"This ruling overrules that lever. The judge pass is now
mandatory; #163's latency finding must be solved with its other levers …
never by dropping SubGroups."* No criterion below touches `--subdivisions`
gating.

**Lever 2 (relay the co-tag screen before generating the reports) is not this
story's decision.** That ordering is decided in the coupled spec group
kogaki#161 / #164 / #166, whose act allowlist fixes the order of the two
permitted post-selection acts. A criterion here would pre-empt that selection.

**Lever 3 is this story**, and the ruling at kogaki#168 already fixes its
scope to cover both composers: *"bound claim/subdivision composition to the
tag-scoped served Gloss shard."*

## Grounding

- `specs/spec-terrain/SPEC.md:867` — "**Tag-scoped and bounded** — one shard
  pair per viewed tag". The shard pair is already the §9 budget; this story
  makes the composer's input match it rather than exceed it.
- `specs/spec-terrain/SPEC.md:617-627` (§7) — the section binds the claim's
  **pinning**, the **gate event** on a subset selection, and the **record's
  shape**, and leaves the wording to the composer. It states no requirement on
  the composer's input, which is why bounding it is inside the existing
  contract rather than an amendment to it.
- `terrain/terrain.mjs:991` `subgroupPlacement` and `:1024` `judgeSubgroup`
  are the subdivision composers reached by the screen path; `:569` `cmdCotags`
  is the screen.

## Acceptance criteria

**AC1 — GroupClaim composition reads the tag-scoped shard, not per-group full
material.**
Given a co-tag run over tag `T` with G groups, when the GroupClaims are
composed, then the material the composer reads is the one tag-scoped served
Gloss shard pair for `T` (`specs/spec-terrain/SPEC.md:867`), fetched once for
the run, and no group's members are re-fetched per group.

**AC2 — SubGroupClaim composition reads the same bounded input.**
Given the same run with subdivision judged (mandatory per kogaki#168), when
SubGroupClaims are composed, then they are composed from the same tag-scoped
shard already fetched for AC1, and no additional per-group material fetch
occurs.

**AC3 — reads scale with candidates, not with placements.**
Given a tag whose placement count exceeds its candidate count (the 2026-08-07
specimen: 131 placements over 70 Lessons), when the co-tag step runs, then the
number of served-material reads is bounded by the candidate count and does not
grow with the placement count.

**AC4 — §7's contract is observably unchanged.**
Given a subset selection after this change, when the claim is recomposed, then
it is re-offered as a gate event and not silently refreshed, and the record
still carries the adopted claim together with the members it was composed
from — `specs/spec-terrain/SPEC.md:617-627` is not weakened by the input bound.

**AC5 — §6.2's judging requirements are unchanged.**
Given the bounded input, when the screen judges its SubGroups, then it still
renders each SubGroup's leaf verdict, still emits both disclosures, and still
refuses without a judge pin (`specs/spec-terrain/SPEC.md:565-582`). A cheaper
input may not buy a quieter judgment.

**AC6 — the latency finding is re-measured, not assumed.**
Given a re-run over the `architecture` tag at comparable size, when the
co-tag step completes, then the PR states the measured interval between the
survey record write and the last Full Report write, against the 2026-08-07
baseline of ~19 minutes. A latency story whose PR reports no number has not
shown its fix worked.

## Story questions — not criteria

- **Does a Gloss-only input change what a GroupClaim may honestly assert?**
  §7 leaves the wording to the composer and states no input requirement, so
  the shipped contract does not answer this. §8's leaf condition requires a
  SubGroupClaim that "composes honestly AND [is] tighter than its parent's" —
  whether a headline-only input can still support the *tighter-than-parent*
  conjunct is **unverified**, and it is raised here as a question rather than
  written as a criterion. If the implementer finds it cannot, that is a
  finding for the spec lane, not a licence to widen the input silently.
- **How much of the ~19 minutes is the judge pass versus claim composition?**
  The issue attributes "likely most of the wall clock" to the judge pass over
  5 groups of up to 27 members. That attribution is the issue's inference and
  was not measured per-phase. AC6's measurement is end-to-end for that reason.
