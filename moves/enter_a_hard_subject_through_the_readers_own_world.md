id: enter_a_hard_subject_through_the_readers_own_world
before: >-
  knowledge: little, maybe the bare word for the subject; question: none, or
  a vague "should I read about this at all?"; expectation: a definition or a
  table of contents; orientation: this is one more book in a crowded genre;
  trust: neutral
after: >-
  knowledge: one claim — interest in the subject rises when the world grows
  unstable; question: "why is everyone reading about this now?" answered,
  "what is unstable now?" opened; expectation: the book will explain
  instability through the subject; orientation: this book knows the genre
  and places itself inside it; trust: raised — the author has admitted
  something against their own interest
question: >-
  holds: none raises: why is everyone reading about this subject now
  settles: why is everyone reading about this subject now raises: what is
  unstable now — left for the book
order: >-
  advances, advances, advances, raises, advances, raises, settles, settles.
  Every foothold lands before the first claim that needs the subject, and
  the question surfaces only once enough footing exists to want it answered.
presupposes: >-
  A shelf recently browsed in a bookstore, and a vague, unexamined sense
  that the world has grown unstable; missing either, the same words read as
  a sales pitch rather than an opening.
technique: >-
  Before the first claim that needs the subject, give the reader three
  things that need none: something they have seen, a map of the kinds of
  text on the subject, and an admission by the author that costs something.
  Then name the subject's central question in the reader's voice and answer
  it in one sentence.
breaks: >-
  remove: the author's opening admission — the genre survey reads as
  promotion and trust does not rise. reorder: put the question before the
  survey — no footing exists yet and no reason to care about the answer.
  extend: add explanatory paragraphs before the question — the first
  specialist claim arrives before the footholds are set and the reader is
  back to expecting a textbook.
draws_on: >-
  the reader's own world: a bookstore shelf recently seen; other texts: a
  sense of the genre's varied formats; author: the author's own admission,
  made against interest; used to survey the field, place the book, and earn
  trust before any specialist term appears.
evidence: >-
  Opening two paragraphs of a Japanese trade-book primer on the subject, in
  English translation.
