id: name_a_paradox_after_removing_aggressive_intent
before: >-
  knowledge: holds a causal model in which a visible outcome is explained by
  an assumed motive behind the actors producing it. question: holds an
  unanswered question about whether that outcome requires the assumed motive
  to be present. expectation: treats a familiar analogy as having already
  finished its explanatory work. orientation: reads the visible outcome as
  evidence of the assumed motive. trust: treats a forthcoming technical term
  as likely to arrive abstract and unearned.
after: >-
  knowledge: knows the outcome can arise without the assumed motive, through
  perception and reciprocal response alone, and can name and define this
  mechanism. question: no longer holds the question open; the mechanism it
  turned on has also been given a name and a compact definition.
  expectation: expects the familiar analogy to keep supporting finer
  distinctions rather than treating it as spent. orientation: reads the
  outcome as a system-level effect obtainable without the assumed motive,
  not as proof of it. trust: trusts the technical term because it was
  reconstructed before being named, rather than asserted first.
question: >-
  holds: does the outcome require the assumed motive to be present in at
  least one actor? settles: does the outcome require the assumed motive to
  be present in at least one actor? — answered negatively, with the causal
  sequence that replaces it specified.
technique: >-
  Retrieve a causal model the reader already accepts, then explicitly strip
  out the one assumption — a hostile or self-interested motive — that reader
  intuition most readily uses to explain its outcome. Show, through
  perception and reciprocal response among the remaining actors, that the
  outcome still follows regardless. Only after the reader has felt that
  persistence firsthand, supply the technical name and a compact,
  transferable definition for the mechanism just demonstrated.
breaks: >-
  remove: dropping the explicit assurance that the assumed motive is absent
  from every actor lets the outcome be explained by that motive after all,
  and the paradox the Move is building toward disappears. reorder: supplying
  the technical name and definition before revisiting the familiar model
  lets the label precede the causal experience that makes its paradox
  intelligible, so the term lands as an assertion instead of a
  reconstruction. extend: adding an actor that retains the removed motive,
  or any other sufficient direct explanation for the outcome, reintroduces a
  cause the Move was built to exclude and weakens the claim that the reduced
  condition alone produces the result.
