id: rescue_the_rule_by_reclassifying_the_apparent_exception
before: >-
  knowledge: a case appears to invert the rule's prediction, leaving the
  rule contradicted by an apparently strong counterexample. question: holds
  an unanswered question about why the case inverts the expected pattern.
  expectation: the next account must either abandon the rule or explain the
  inversion. orientation: classifies cases by an aggregate, single-dimension
  ranking. trust: the rule risks appearing immunized against a clear
  counterexample.
after: >-
  knowledge: the contradiction is traced to a misclassified case; the
  classification is revised using the direction and membership of observed
  responses to it, restoring the rule's prediction. question: sharpened to
  why the ranking used to classify the case and the responses actually
  observed toward it diverge. expectation: the rule stays intact while the
  classification criterion is revised to admit further variables.
  orientation: classification becomes relational, inferred from how other
  cases respond to a candidate rather than read off a single ranking. trust:
  partly restored by a premise-level diagnosis tied to observable responses,
  with the deeper causal account honestly deferred.
question: >-
  holds: why does the case invert the rule's predicted pattern? settles: the
  rule itself is not wrong. replaces: the case's classification is wrong
  with what the correct classification is, inferred from observed responses
  to it. raises: why does the ranking that produced the misclassification
  diverge from what other cases' responses reveal.
technique: >-
  Concede that an observation appears to violate a rule, then preserve the
  rule by relocating the fault to the classification of the case rather than
  to the rule itself. Infer the corrected classification from the direction
  and membership of other cases' observable responses, using their behavior
  as evidence for what the case truly is rather than what its raw ranking
  suggested. Once the contradiction is repaired, restate the remaining
  mismatch as a sharper question and name the additional variables the next
  explanation will need.
breaks: >-
  remove: without a concrete observable response tying the replacement
  candidates to the classification, the reclassification loses its strongest
  evidence and reads as assertion. reorder: raising the sharper question
  before completing the reclassification leaves the reader inside the
  original contradiction instead of watching the rule get repaired first.
  extend: supplying the full deeper explanation immediately collapses the
  reclassification, the new question, and the promised explanatory pivot
  into one move, losing the staged effect of repair-then-reopen.
