id: distinguish_two_periods_on_two_axes
before: >-
  knowledge: knows that both periods contained serious conflict, without a
  basis for telling them apart. question: holds an unformed question about
  what changed besides the sheer count of cases. expectation: expects
  renewed interest to be explained by a greater quantity of conflict.
  orientation: treats the later period as a continuation of the earlier
  one's violence.
after: >-
  knowledge: can distinguish the periods along two independent axes — what
  the cases concerned and who acted in them — anchored by a named example
  for each. question: holds a new question about why the change moved from
  nonstate and smaller actors toward territorial great-power rivalry.
  expectation: expects the renewed interest to be explained by a fit between
  a changed kind of case and a particular explanatory lens, not by volume.
  orientation: treats the later period as a qualitative break on two axes at
  once.
question: >-
  holds: what changed besides the number of cases. replaces: what changed
  besides the number of cases with why did the object and the actors of
  conflict both shift toward great-power territorial rivalry.
order: >-
  advances, advances, raises, advances, settles, settles — the earlier
  period is completed on both axes before the contrast is announced, then
  the contrast moves from classification to named cases to a compressed
  synthesis to its consequence; each step supplies what the next needs
  rather than restating what came before.
presupposes: >-
  the reader can recognize the named examples used to anchor each axis as
  belonging to the category they are offered under.
technique: >-
  Describe an earlier period on two independent axes — what the cases
  concerned and who was involved. Contrast a later period on the same two
  axes, ground the contrast in one or two recognizable examples, compress
  both axis-changes into a single sentence, and only then derive why a
  previously peripheral explanatory lens has become the fitting one.
breaks: >-
  remove: dropping either axis collapses the contrast into a change in
  subject matter alone, and the claim resting on both axes together loses
  its support. reorder: stating the compressed two-axis conclusion before
  the named examples leaves the classification as an unsupported abstraction
  instead of a recognizable pattern. extend: adding further cases to either
  period buries the paired contrast under a catalogue and erodes the balance
  between the two axes.
