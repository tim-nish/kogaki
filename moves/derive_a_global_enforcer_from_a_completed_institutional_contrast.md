before: >-
  knowledge: attributes persistent conflict mainly to interests, identities,
  memories, or human nature. question: holds an unanswered question about
  why the phenomenon persists. orientation: treats the problem as one of
  motive among the actors involved. trust: a proposed universal remedy would
  sound implausibly sweeping if stated outright.
after: >-
  knowledge: locates the missing element as an enforcing institution rather
  than a missing rule or missing motive, and can name the higher authority
  that would supply it. question: the operational question of who can compel
  compliance is answered; whether the derived institution can actually be
  created remains open. orientation: treats the problem as a structural
  consequence of rules existing without a superior enforcer, alongside the
  original motive-based view. trust: raised by having independently
  reconstructed the missing function before being told the remedy, and by a
  recognized authority having reached the same conclusion under comparable
  stakes.
technique: >-
  Open with a broad causal question and grant its familiar range of answers,
  then replace it with a narrower operational question about compelled
  restraint. Build a complete functional model of enforcement in a domain
  where it already exists, transpose that same model onto a domain lacking
  one of its parts, show the behavioral consequence of the missing part, and
  derive the institution that would supply it — closing with an independent
  authority who reached the same derivation, so the conclusion lands as
  confirmed rather than introduced.
breaks: >-
  remove: dropping the familiar-domain model leaves the derived institution
  looking like an unsupported leap rather than a completed parallel.
  reorder: citing the authoritative precedent before the domains are
  compared makes authority substitute for the derivation instead of
  confirming one the reader has already reached. extend: inserting
  alternative remedies or feasibility objections before stating the derived
  conclusion blurs the comparison's deliberate completeness and weakens the
  sense of an inevitable answer.
