id: reframe_a_causal_question_as_an_enforcement_gap
before: >-
  knowledge: persistent conflict or disorder may be explained by a range of
  familiar motives, interests, or dispositions. question: holds an
  unanswered question about why the phenomenon persists. expectation: the
  explanation will choose among familiar motives for the phenomenon.
  orientation: the problem is primarily about what motivates the behavior.
  trust: baseline, no signal yet about how the explanation will proceed.
after: >-
  knowledge: restraint in a domain depends on an authority that both holds
  and exercises the power to stop unauthorized action, not merely on a rule
  that forbids it. question: the narrower question is settled in a familiar
  domain and reopened, unanswered, in an unfamiliar comparison domain.
  expectation: the explanation will compare the institutions that govern
  restraint across domains rather than continue surveying motives.
  orientation: the problem is also about whether any superior authority can
  enforce restraint, not only about what drives the behavior. trust: raised,
  because several plausible motive-based answers were granted before the
  narrower question displaced them.
question: >-
  holds: why does the phenomenon persist across cases?; replaces: why does
  the phenomenon persist across cases? with who has the power to compel
  restraint, and does the case in question have it?; settles: who has the
  power to compel restraint, answered for the familiar domain; raises: who
  performs the equivalent function in the unfamiliar comparison domain.
order: >-
  raises / advances / advances / advances / settles. The broad question is
  opened and populated with its familiar range of answers before being set
  aside for a narrower one, so the narrowing reads as a deliberate move past
  known ground rather than an evasion of it; the familiar domain is then
  walked step by step from rule to enforcing power so that the principle the
  comparison needs is derived, not asserted.
presupposes: >-
  the reader already knows the ordinary sequence of rule-violation-then-
  enforcement in the familiar domain well enough to recognize it without
  further explanation, and accepts that domain as a legitimate analogy for
  the unfamiliar one.
technique: >-
  Open with the broadest form of a causal question and grant the range of
  answers the reader already holds, so the survey reads as complete rather
  than dismissed. Replace that question with a narrower operational one
  about enforcement capacity, then work through a domain the reader already
  trusts, separating the existence of a rule from the power that makes it
  effective. Compress that domain's example into a single institutional
  principle, which stands ready to be carried, unanswered, into the
  unfamiliar domain the reframing was aimed at.
breaks: >-
  remove: dropping the initial survey of familiar answers makes the
  narrowing read as suppression of alternatives rather than a considered
  move past them. reorder: presenting the enforcement question before the
  familiar-domain walkthrough turns the walkthrough into unmotivated civics
  rather than the deliberately built half of a comparison. extend: adding
  qualifications, exceptions, or failure cases to the familiar domain before
  the comparison closes shifts attention to that domain's legitimacy and
  stalls the transfer to the unfamiliar one.
draws_on: >-
  the reader's own world: the familiar domain (an institution the reader has
  direct or ordinary experience of) supplies the worked example from which
  the enforcement principle is derived, rather than the technique asserting
  that principle directly.
