id: qualify_static_advantage_through_counteradaptation
status: observed
intent: >-
  Show that an apparent advantage is temporary because opposing actors can
  adapt to the mechanism producing it.
requires: >-
  The reader understands why one side holds an advantage under the current
  conditions but has not considered the opponent's response.
effect: >-
  The reader understands the advantage as contingent and capable of erosion
  when countermeasures target its underlying mechanism.
constraints: >-
  The countermeasure must respond causally to the source of the advantage;
  complete or lasting equilibrium must not be assumed without support.
failure_modes: >-
  Treating any later change as counteradaptation; omitting how the response
  weakens the advantage; or presenting eventual balance as inevitable.
sources: >-
  Observed in "Attack-Defense Advantage Is Ambiguous." Artillery first
  shifts siege warfare toward attack, after which defenders adopt stone
  construction, deeper and wider moats, elevated observation, and defensive
  artillery that reduce the original advantage.
