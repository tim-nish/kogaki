id: derive_balance_of_power_from_reciprocal_self_help
before: >-
  knowledge: an institutional absence is established but its practical
  consequence for behavior is not yet drawn out. question: holds an
  unanswered question about what follows in practice from the absence.
  expectation: expects the next step to evaluate or extend the institutional
  diagnosis itself. orientation: treats greater strength as a plausible
  unilateral route to safety. trust: holds a claim about collective behavior
  at some remove, before it has been made concrete.
after: >-
  knowledge: the absence forces self-help, and self-help by one party
  necessarily degrades another's security, so unilateral strengthening
  provokes a matching response. question: settles why actors seek strength
  and why interaction tends toward balance; leaves how stable any particular
  balance will be open. expectation: expects the account to proceed on the
  working condition that no external enforcer exists. orientation: treats
  strength as relational — protective for the holder, threatening to the
  neighbor, and liable to provoke counteraction. trust: raised by having
  walked the mechanism through a fully worked familiar case rather than
  asserting it of the abstract actors directly.
question: >-
  holds: what follows in practice from the absence of a guaranteeing
  authority? settles: why actors seek strength and why their interaction
  tends toward a balance rather than one-sided accumulation.
order: >-
  raises, advances, advances, advances, advances, advances, advances,
  settles, advances, settles — the reciprocal escalation must complete once
  inside the familiar scene before the mechanism is exported and named in
  the target domain, so the reader has already felt the equilibrium form
  before being asked to see it as a general principle.
presupposes: >-
  the reader accepts that no external authority reliably enforces order
  among the actors in question.
technique: >-
  Take an established institutional absence and, instead of arguing its
  consequence abstractly, restate it as a needed self-protective act, then
  drop the reader into a small familiar scene where that act is taken by one
  party. Introduce a second party who reasons identically in response to the
  first, and let the two rounds of matching response run until a stopping
  condition appears on its own. Only after the mechanism has fully played
  out in the familiar scene, transfer it wholesale to the target domain and
  name the resulting condition.
breaks: >-
  remove: without the second party's matching response the technique yields
  only an individual solution and never produces a system-level equilibrium.
  reorder: introducing the target-domain terminology before the familiar
  scene's reciprocal sequence completes interrupts the derivation and turns
  it into an assertion instead of a demonstration. extend: adding further
  variables (intentions, alliances, misperception) into the reciprocal
  sequence obscures the minimal chain from shared insecurity to equilibrium
  that the technique depends on.
draws_on: >-
  the reader's own world — a domestic scenario of theft, weapons, and a
  neighbor — supplies the mechanism the target domain's actors are then
  shown to share.
evidence: >-
  geopolitics primer analyzed as
  derive_balance_of_power_from_reciprocal_self_help; passage running from
  the "no world police" premise through the Japan–Russia power-definition
  example.
