id: trace_reciprocal_escalation_from_ambiguous_signal
status: observed
intent: >-
  Trace how an ambiguous action prompts defensive responses that each side
  interprets as further reason to escalate.
requires: >-
  The reader understands an action whose intent cannot be reliably
  determined.
effect: >-
  The reader understands how uncertainty can become a self-reinforcing cycle
  of suspicion, defensive response, and escalation.
constraints: >-
  Each response must affect the other side's perception; a sequence of
  unrelated increases does not constitute reciprocal escalation.
failure_modes: >-
  Listing escalation without showing the feedback loop; assigning hostile
  intent where the actors face uncertainty; or treating escalation as
  inevitable.
sources: >-
  Observed in "Why Is a Weak State Like North Korea So Frightening?" The
  passage explains how unclear military expansion leads other states to arm
  defensively, producing mutual suspicion and further expansion. It later
  applies the same loop to responses to unpredictable North Korean behavior.
