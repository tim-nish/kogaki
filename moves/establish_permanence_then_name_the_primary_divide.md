id: establish_permanence_then_name_the_primary_divide
before: >-
  knowledge: several important forces may appear to vary on comparable
  historical time scales. question: holds an unresolved sense of why a
  condition among many should reveal policy across long periods.
  expectation: anticipates a continuing list of important influences.
  orientation: treats the subject as one influence among several, alongside
  technology, ideas, and other historical variables. trust: holds the claim
  of foundational importance as plausible but broad.
after: >-
  knowledge: one condition is nearly fixed relative to the others, can
  inform long-term policy, and divides cases into two primary types.
  question: the reader now holds an open question of how particular cases
  belong to one of the two types. expectation: anticipates that later
  explanation will be organized around a durable two-part division and will
  classify individual cases through it. orientation: treats the subject as a
  stable foundation for understanding policy and as the source of the
  central classification. trust: raised by convergence among an independent
  cultural reference, a theorist, and a practical authority.
question: >-
  raises: why should this one condition, among several important influences,
  reveal something durable about policy? settles: by its comparative
  permanence, then by naming the resulting two-part division as the answer.
order: >-
  advances, advances, advances, advances, advances, advances, raises,
  settles, settles, advances. The premise accumulates through comparison,
  cultural image, and converging authorities before the reader is given the
  question it was building toward, and the classification follows as the
  question's answer rather than preceding it.
presupposes: >-
  the reader recognizes the named cultural reference as a shared point of
  memory, and accepts the named authorities as credible on the relation
  between geography and policy.
technique: >-
  Establish a property's durability by contrasting its time scale against
  other important influences that change faster, then make that duration
  felt through a familiar cultural image. Layer independent theoretical and
  practical authorities onto the same claim so their agreement itself poses
  the question of what matters most, then answer that question with a
  compact binary division grounded in recognizable cases.
breaks: >-
  remove: dropping the cultural image leaves expert authority intact but
  loses the reader's own felt sense of the property's durability. reorder:
  presenting the binary division before establishing permanence turns the
  classification into an assertion instead of a conclusion the reader has
  been led to. extend: piling on further authorities before posing the
  central question delays the turn and dilutes the force of the
  question-answer transition.
draws_on: >-
  the reader's own world: a shared cultural memory makes an abstract
  contrast felt rather than merely stated. other texts: a poem, a theorist,
  and a practical-political figure are drawn on as independent, converging
  witnesses to the same claim.
