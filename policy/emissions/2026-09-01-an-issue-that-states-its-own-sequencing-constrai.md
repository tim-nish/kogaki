## Trigger — what happened

kogaki#743 asked for a 2,296-line spec to be slimmed to ~600 lines, and its own Scope section said 'sequenced with or after this batch's design issues so §6/§13 are not rewritten twice'. The owner then invoked /ship-cycle on it directly. All six sibling design issues were open; two of them amended §6 and §13 by number, and a third moved the three carrier files the surviving precedence map is written about. Running it would have rewritten the document's largest keep section before its subjects moved.

## The learning

A direct invocation names WHICH item to work, and says nothing about whether that item's own stated preconditions hold — so it is not a waiver of them, and reading it as one converts the issue's most load-bearing sentence into decoration. The tell is specific: the constraint is written in the issue by the person who filed it, precisely because they knew something at filing time that the run does not re-derive. So a pickup reads the scope section for preconditions before classifying, and where one is unmet the honest terminal state is a park whose trigger is the precondition itself. That is also the test for whether the park is healthy rather than an abandonment — a precondition stated as a set of issue numbers has a mechanical observer, which is exactly the property a hold trigger owes.
