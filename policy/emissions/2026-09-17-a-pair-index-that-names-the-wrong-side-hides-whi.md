## Trigger — what happened

A review item asked one question per item the READER produced, matched to the closest thing the plan declared. It failed on every passage of the first real run, because the plan tells the writer to retell material it makes no claim about — so producing more than the plan declares is correct by design. A companion check that would have caught the opposite problem, a planned point the reader never got, had no consequence attached and reported nothing anyone acted on.

## The learning

When you check whether work carried something through, ask the question once per thing that was PLANNED, not once per thing that came out. Asking per output makes surplus look like a defect, and surplus is usually the point. It also loses the only answer that matters to whoever fixes it: which planned thing went missing. Carry the planned item's own words on each answer, so the repair instruction can name it instead of naming the check that failed. And when one check detects surplus and a second detects loss, notice that the one with teeth is usually the wrong one.
