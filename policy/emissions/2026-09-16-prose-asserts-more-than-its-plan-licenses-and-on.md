## Trigger — what happened

A reverse-outlining review of a five-part article: a reader who had never seen the plan wrote down what each passage claimed, and on every one of the five parts they recovered more claims than the plan had licensed. All five were rewritten against that finding, re-read by fresh readers, and all five failed the same item again. Six of the six surviving items are that one shape.

## The learning

When a plan lists what a piece of work may assert and the work is then written well, the writing carries more than the list. The extra is not padding: it is the connective material that makes prose readable — the sentence explaining why the previous point follows, the one naming what kind of thing is being discussed, the one telling the reader where they now stand. A reader reconstructing the plan from the finished text cannot tell that material from the licensed claims, because on the page it has the same grammatical form as an assertion. So an instrument that compares a reader's reconstruction against the declared list will report an overreach on every well-written part, and will keep reporting it after a correction, because removing the connective material is the one repair that would make the text worse. The useful reading of a finding that survives a correction pass on every single unit is not that every unit is defective; it is that the instrument is measuring the difference between a list of claims and a piece of writing, which is a real difference and not a fault. Two things follow. A plan that licenses claims should say whether connective material is inside the licence or outside it, because the checker cannot infer that. And when a finding recurs uniformly across every unit, look at the instrument before ordering another round of repairs: uniformity is evidence about the measure, while a defect would be uneven.
