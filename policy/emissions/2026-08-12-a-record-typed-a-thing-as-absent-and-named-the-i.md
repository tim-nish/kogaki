## Trigger — what happened

A record typed a thing as absent and named the issue where it was owed. An hour later half of that thing landed — the ratified grammar, not the acting code — and nothing walked the enumeration to say so. The record was still literally true, since the acting half was genuinely missing, and it had stopped being useful: a reader learned the observer was unbuilt and did not learn that the design for it was now in the tree. The enumeration's own widening trigger did not fire, because what landed added a declared line class rather than a new row, and changed what no existing observer reads.

## The learning

A record that types something as absent goes stale the moment its subject becomes PARTLY present, and it goes stale while still being true — which is why nothing catches it. The absence claim survives, because the part that is missing really is missing; what dies is the reader's ability to see how much of the thing now exists. Triggers built to re-run such a typing are usually written to fire on the whole subject arriving, so a half-landing slips past every one of them. Two things follow. When you land half of something a record calls absent, go and complete that record's prose in the same act, because you are the only party who knows the halves came apart. And keep the TYPE where it is: the honest move is to name the carrier that landed and refuse to assert the state of the part that did not, since promoting the type on a half-arrival is the false record the typed absence exists to prevent.
