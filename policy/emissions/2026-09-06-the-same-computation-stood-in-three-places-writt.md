## Trigger — what happened

The same computation stood in three places: written inline twice in one command's two reports, and a third time, differently and wrongly, in another command that keyed on the coarser unit. Only the third was defective, and it had been reviewed and merged.

## The learning

A duplicated computation does not fail uniformly — the copies that sit beside each other stay in step because whoever edits one sees the other, and the copy that drifts is the one written at a DIFFERENT altitude in a different function, where it reads as that function's own local answer rather than as a copy of anything. So the tell is not duplication count but a shared question answered at two granularities. When such a computation is folded into one definition, the reuse claim is worth asserting rather than stating: a mutation that narrows the single definition and shows EVERY reader failing at once is what distinguishes a genuinely shared definition from three call sites that happen to agree today.
