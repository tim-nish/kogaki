## Trigger — what happened

A review found that a check's admission record claimed a judgment the code did not perform. The repair added a clause naming the narrowing explicitly — and, three lines below it in the same field of the same commit, added a second sentence claiming a new guard covered every key when it covered four of six. The next review round found the second overstatement and named it as the class the clause three lines above had just been added to repair.

## The learning

Repairing an unbacked claim puts the author in the one posture where writing another is easiest: they have just proven to themselves that they are the kind of author who checks, and the proof was earned on the sentence beside the one they are now writing. The correction is treated as covering the field rather than the sentence, so the new text arrives inheriting a credibility the old text spent.

What makes it structural rather than careless is the ORDER OF WORK. The repair is written last, after the mechanism it describes is built and passing, at the point where attention has moved from what the code does to how the record reads. And the new claim is usually a generalization of something true — here a guard that really did close the route, over a set smaller than the sentence implied — so it fails no test and reads as a summary rather than as an assertion.

Two things follow. When a review finding is that a record overstates, the repair covers the WHOLE FIELD and not the flagged sentence: re-derive every claim in it against the code as it now stands, because the field is what the reader trusts and the flagged sentence was only where the defect was noticed. And a claim of the form 'X is checked' owes the SET — which X, computed from the same source the mechanism computes from, never a list typed beside it. A hand-written list of members is the artifact that goes stale between the round that writes it and the round that reads it, which is a short enough interval to observe directly.

The general shape: a correction is authored in the state of mind least able to audit itself, immediately adjacent to the text that just failed an audit.
