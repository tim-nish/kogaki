## Trigger — what happened

A paragraph was written to correct a misattributed quotation elsewhere in the same document. Inside it, quoting the source's warning, the author capitalised one word for emphasis — leaving the emphasis inside the quotation marks. The file does use caps for emphasis, so the intent was legible, but the altered word sat in quotes in the paragraph whose subject is that unmarked edits hide behind the label that tells a reader not to check.

## The learning

Emphasis added inside quotation marks is an edit to the quotation, however conventional the emphasis is in the surrounding document. The house style that makes it readable is exactly what makes it invisible: you are not conscious of having changed anything, because the change is formatting rather than words. Put emphasis outside the quotation, or mark it as added. And note where this is most likely — writing about fidelity puts you in a register of strong assertion, which is the register that reaches for emphasis, so the sentence explaining why quotations must be exact is the sentence most likely to decorate one.
