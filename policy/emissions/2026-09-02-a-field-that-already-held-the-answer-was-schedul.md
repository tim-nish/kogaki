## Trigger — what happened

A record schema carried a field named for what an early implementation had appended to it (a loading route and tags), not for what it held. After that contamination was cleaned out, the field contained the author's own short account of the passage they had observed. A later ruling asked for an 'excerpt' in each record, and the implementing sitting read the word as a verbatim quotation, found no source text in reach, declared the work blocked on material it could not fabricate, and scheduled a re-extraction of all twenty-two records. The owner then said the field was already the excerpt, that a verbatim quotation was never wanted because pasting a long article into a record is noise, and that the only change owed was renaming the field. The repair was a one-key rename plus withdrawing the blocked errand.

## The learning

When a ruling names a thing the records already seem to lack, check whether they hold it under another name before scheduling work to produce it. A field's name is a record of the design that created it, and after a cleanup the name can lag the content by a whole design. The tell here was a rename that everyone agreed was harmless and a re-extraction that nobody could perform: the impossible half of a plan is often the half built on a misread word. Two habits follow. First, when a term like 'excerpt' or 'source' arrives in a ruling, ask what the owner wants a later reader to be able to do with it, because that decides whether verbatim text or an author's account is the right content, and the two are not interchangeable. Second, an honest refusal to fabricate can still rest on a wrong premise, and the refusal being correct does not make the premise correct; the sitting that refuses should say what it believed it was refusing, so the premise can be checked by someone who knows better.
