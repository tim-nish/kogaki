## Trigger — what happened

A record required a note saying whether a question had actually been asked or was written up afterwards. The note was added correctly, in the required form, saying the question was composed after the fact. Then, wanting to make it stronger, the author also ran the question, and added that it had been run — quoting the reference number of the run and its outcome. But the run was not recorded anywhere a reader could check; only the two earlier consultations were. So the record now carried an identifier and a verdict with nothing behind them, in the exact field that exists to let a reader tell a real query from a composed one. A reviewer independently re-ran the question and confirmed the claim was true — the problem was that nothing in the record could have shown it either way.

## The learning

Adding a stronger claim to a record can weaken the record, when the stronger claim has no evidence attached and the weaker one did. The required disclosure was satisfied and conforming; what broke it was the surplus — an identifier and an outcome quoted in prose, which look exactly like a verified reference and are not one. That is worse than saying less, because a reader who sees a reference number reasonably stops checking. So when you strengthen a record, ask what a reader would have to do to falsify the new part, and if the answer is nothing, either attach the evidence in whatever form the record verifies, or leave the weaker claim standing alone. The tell is a citation added to make something more convincing rather than because the citation was already there — the motive is presentational and the effect is on trust.
