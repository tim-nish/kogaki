## Trigger — what happened

An owner ruling removed a member count from a heading in a rendered screen. That count was the denominator one format rule divided by and one side of the comparison another made. Implementing the ruling as written would have left both rules declared, both executed on every render, and neither able to evaluate anything — passing forever.

## The learning

A format rule reads specific tokens out of the text it governs, and a ruling about how that text should look is not usually thought of as touching the rules. But the ruling and the rule meet at the token: delete it and the rule still loads, still runs, still reports no violation, and the silence is indistinguishable from a clean result. Nothing fails, so nothing announces the loss.

The repair is not to find a substitute token and point the rule at it. Where the surface still carries the pieces the rule needs — several smaller counts that add up to the one that was removed — the rule can be genuinely re-instrumented, and it keeps measuring what it was ratified to measure. Where it does not, re-pointing produces a comparison of a quantity against itself: a predicate that cannot fail, which is worse than an absent rule because it occupies the space where one would be noticed missing. Such a rule is better withdrawn, in writing, with the property it carried re-sited to wherever it can still be tested and the coverage that move costs stated as a loss rather than as an equivalent.

The practical form: when a shape ruling lands, read every rule that consumes the shape before implementing it, and treat a rule that would go quiet as part of the ruling's cost rather than as a detail discovered afterwards. A rule whose only remaining form is a tautology has stopped being a rule, and saying so is the honest outcome.
