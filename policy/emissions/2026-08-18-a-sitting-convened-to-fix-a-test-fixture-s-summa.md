## Trigger — what happened

A sitting convened to fix a test fixture's summary line — it claimed the fixture established a guard that only a different layer actually checked — shipped a repair whose own added evidence was already counted elsewhere under a different description, so the repaired total still did not reconcile.

## The learning

When you repair a claim that overstated its evidence, the repair almost always adds evidence of its own: a reproduction, a demonstration, a worked example. That new evidence is the most likely place for a second description of one fact to enter, because everyone is reading the repair for whether it is TRUE and nobody is reading it for whether it DOUBLE-COUNTS.

Here the repair correctly named the layer that holds the guard, and demonstrated it by execution — disable the guard, this fixture still passes. That demonstration was a re-run of a mutation the summary already counted under another name, so a reader adding up the named items could reach one more than the stated total. The identical defect the sitting was convened to fix, reproduced inside the fix.

The review found it and filed it as a background note rather than as something to correct, on the reasonable ground that it was small. Correcting it was right anyway: an artifact whose whole licence is 'this total must reconcile' cannot ship with a total that still does not. Where a defect lives inside the change's own text, the change is its carrier — severity is not what decides that, and a background note is where a small correct finding goes to be forgotten.

So: before shipping a repair to an evidence claim, enumerate what the repair itself now asserts, and check each item against what the artifact already counts.
