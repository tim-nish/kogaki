## Trigger — what happened

A durable clause quoted a rule, cited the rule's pin, and then broke that rule twice in its own next two sentences — pinning another repository's spec at a version number and predicting how long its behaviour would last, when the rule quoted says to name a carrier and never assert its state.

## The learning

Citing a rule is not the same as applying it, and the gap is widest right where you cite. Quoting a constraint puts you in the frame of having satisfied it, so the sentences immediately around the quotation are the least examined text you will write — and they are the sentences most likely to be about the very thing the rule governs, because that is why you reached for the quote. When you quote a rule into a document, read the paragraph you wrote around it back against the rule specifically, as if someone else had written it. The version pin and the forward-looking clause are the two shapes to look for: both feel like precision and both are claims about state you do not observe.
