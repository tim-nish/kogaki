## Trigger — what happened

A policy verdict was posted judging a proposed fix, and its text read the remedy as one that removes a duplicate and adds no detector. An hour later the same session implemented the fix and added a 36-line mechanical counter, arguing the issue's remedy line licensed it. A reviewer found the contradiction by reading the issue whole, including the verdict the session itself had written.

## The learning

The record you write while judging a piece of work is the one you are least likely to re-read while doing it. A verdict, a classification, an acceptance note — these get composed in a judging frame and then the implementing frame starts from the task, not from what was promised about it. So the artifact that authorised the work is exactly the artifact nobody checks the work against. Before finishing anything you earlier judged or scoped in writing, re-open that writing and read it as a spec for what you just built. And notice the sharper version: if you cited a principle in the verdict, check whether what you built is the thing that principle warns about, because invoking a rule does not inoculate you against breaking it.
