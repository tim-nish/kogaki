## Trigger — what happened

In a two-sided review, a per-unit judge is asked whether a passage re-explains something an earlier passage already established, while the same judge is instructed to look at one unit in isolation and never at its neighbours. Two of those judgments came back as honest non-answers saying exactly that, and a third answered the opposite of what its own stated reason supported.

## The learning

A judging question whose subject spans two units cannot be answered by a judge scoped to one. The isolation that keeps the judge from ranking is the same isolation that starves this particular question, so the answer arrives as a non-answer or, worse, as a confident token pointing the other way from its own reason. When scoping a judge, check every question against the scope: a cross-unit question needs its evidence packaged into the single unit the judge is handed, not a reminder to consider context it is forbidden to look at.
