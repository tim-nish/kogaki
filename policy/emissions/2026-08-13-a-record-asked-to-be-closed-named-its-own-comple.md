## Trigger — what happened

A record asked to be closed named its own completion test: a search string should stop appearing in a document. The change was made and the search string still appeared twice — once in the document's own history of the change, and once in a quotation of the old wording that the house rule on amendments requires be kept. The underlying thing the test stood for was genuinely fixed.

## The learning

A completion test that searches for a phrase can be defeated by the very rule that says old wording must be preserved rather than erased. The phrase survives in the record of its own removal, so the test reports the work as unfinished forever, and the only way to satisfy it is to delete the history it was never meant to touch. When a document's convention is to keep superseded wording beside the new wording, a test that counts occurrences of that wording is measuring something adjacent to the real question rather than the question itself. Write the test against the thing that actually changed - the live entry and what it now says - not against the absence of a string from the whole file.
