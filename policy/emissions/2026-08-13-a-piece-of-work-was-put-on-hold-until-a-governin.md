## Trigger — what happened

A piece of work was put on hold until a governing body answered a question about where a certain responsibility belonged: with instrument A or with instrument B. The answer, when it came, was that instrument B had been retired entirely. The hold's condition was met, but nothing about the original question got answered — it stopped applying.

## The learning

A hold written as a choice between named options assumes those options will still exist when the answer arrives, and sometimes what resolves it is one of them disappearing. That is a different outcome from the question being decided, and it wants a different response: resuming as if the fork had been settled would pick a side of something that no longer has sides. Worth checking at the moment a hold lifts: did the answer decide the question, or dissolve it? The second case is easy to mis-read as the first, because both look like the condition being satisfied. There is a further step that is easy to skip. When the original objection dissolves, the held work is not automatically owed — some other reason for waiting may have been true all along and simply never needed stating, because the first objection was doing the work. So before resuming, ask why the work was not done, rather than only whether the recorded blocker has cleared. If a different reason now stands, write it down where the next reader will meet it, because the record still names the objection that expired.
