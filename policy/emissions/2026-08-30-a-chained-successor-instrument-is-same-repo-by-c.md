## Trigger — what happened

kogaki#657 is an owner-commissioned design whose every acceptance criterion is harness behaviour living in a different repository. The ship-cycle run correctly identified the Blocker, took the owner-gated approval, and invoked the chained-successor mint with the only repository flag the subcommand offers. That flag names the repository of the WHOLE operation - predecessor and successor together - so the successor landed in the consumer repo carrying a body written for the harness repo, opening with the words 'why this issue exists here and not in kogaki' at the moment it existed in kogaki.

## The learning

A succession instrument that takes one repository argument encodes an assumption its own vocabulary hides: that a remainder belongs where its predecessor does. That holds for every ordinary case and fails exactly where the remainder is most likely to be lost - work assigned to a role whose addressee is a different repository, which is the case the close coupling exists to protect. The failure is quiet because every mechanical part succeeds: the approval records, the mint runs, the chain declaration is written, the predecessor stays open, and the only thing wrong is the destination, which no field names and no check reads. What the situation actually needs is TWO links, and recognising that is the durable part: the chain link stays in the predecessor's repository because that is where the close coupling must key, and a separate issue is filed in the repository that owns the act, because an obligation with no carrier in the repository that must act on it dies silently. Naming the two-link shape at the moment of the mis-landing is what stops the next sitting either forcing a one-link mint that cannot reach, or dropping the chain link and leaving the predecessor closable against live work. The general rule: before invoking any instrument that takes a single scope argument, ask whether the thing being created belongs in the same scope as the thing it is created from - and where a subcommand offers one flag for two roles, treat the missing distinction as the finding rather than as a usage error.
