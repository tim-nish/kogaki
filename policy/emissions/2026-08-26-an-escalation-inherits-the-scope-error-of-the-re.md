## Trigger — what happened

A sitting stopped work and escalated a design fork, reporting that removing two entry points would strand fourteen registered assertions. The claim came from a prior sitting's stop report, which had named both entry points together; the child issue quoted it and the escalation quoted the child. The next sitting read the ratified table and found one of the two entry points was removed by nothing — it was listed as live and bound to a state. The real exposure was two lines and mechanical. Three artifacts had carried the error forward, each one accurately quoting the one before it.

## The learning

A stop report is written at the moment of least information — the work has just failed and the author is explaining why — and everything downstream treats it as the finding rather than as a claim. Its scope sentence is the part most likely to be wrong, because a stop is triggered by hitting ONE obstacle and the sentence generalizes from that one to a set the author has not enumerated. Downstream artifacts then quote it faithfully, which is exactly what makes the error durable: each restatement is honest, sourced, and adds a layer of apparent corroboration while adding no evidence.

The tell is a claim about a SET whose members are individually checkable and were not individually checked. Here it was two entry points named together and one count of assertions; the ratified table listing which entry points are removed was one read away and no artifact in the chain had made it. Quoting a report is not verifying it, and a chain of accurate quotations of an unverified claim reads exactly like corroboration.

So the rule is at the ESCALATION rather than at the report: an escalation re-establishes the scope claim it is escalating, against the artifact that governs it, before spending anyone's decision on it. This is cheap — the governing artifact is what the escalation is about — and it is the last point where the error costs only a read. Past it, the cost is a decision made about a fork that does not exist, and every alternative weighed for it is weighed for nothing.

The rider worth keeping: the stop itself was correct. Stopping on an obstacle is right even when the obstacle is smaller than it looked, and the correction is owed to the SCOPE SENTENCE, not to the judgment that stopped.
