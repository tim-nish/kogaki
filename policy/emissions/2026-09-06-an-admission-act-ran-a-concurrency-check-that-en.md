## Trigger — what happened

An admission act ran a concurrency check that ended with a hint: this issue declares no footprint, so it licenses nothing and intersects nothing — record one with 'admit-issue verdict --plan-cell files=...'. The verdict call was made exactly as instructed and was refused: a plan shape was supplied, but the chain's disposition is unevaluated, which carries no implementation licence, so nothing the shape could be measured against exists. Dropping the flag the hint asked for made the same call succeed. The issue was then implemented and merged with no footprint recorded anywhere, which is the state the hint was written to prevent.

## The learning

One act's remedy line can name a flag a second act refuses, and neither act is wrong on its own: the checker correctly reports that an absence of evidence is not evidence of disjointness, and the admitter correctly refuses to attach a plan shape to a disposition that licenses no pull request. What is missing is that neither knows the other's precondition, so the advice is unconditional while the acceptance is not. The cost is quiet — the operator does the thing they were told, gets a refusal, does the thing that works, and the gap the advice existed to close stays open with nothing recording that it was attempted. Where one act advises an act another gates, the advice owes the gate's condition or the gate owes the advice's case; an advisory line that cannot be followed in the state it is printed in is worse than no line, because it spends the reader's attention and leaves them believing the remedy was refused on its merits.
