## Trigger — what happened

A record schema forbade a list of verdict tokens at every depth — holds, fails, verdict, score — so that a blind reviewer could not smuggle a judgment into a recovered record. A new conditional field was added whose three sub-fields described what a reader takes from a figure, and the third was named 'holds': what the reader holds after looking. Every record carrying the new field was then refused as a reviewer writing a verdict. The refusal named the reviewer and the repair the reviewer could not make, because the reviewer had written the field the table asked for.

## The learning

A prohibition keyed on a bare token reaches every future field that happens to spell one, and the collision is invisible to whoever adds the field because they are reading their own field's meaning rather than the prohibition's list. The failure lands on the wrong party: the refusal is composed for the actor the guard was written to catch, so the honest actor is told they did something they did not do and pointed at a repair that is not theirs. Two things follow. A new field name is checked against the guard vocabularies its record already lives under, not only against the fields beside it — a homonym in a join key costs what a divergence costs. And where a name reads naturally in the new field's own terms, the tell that it is a homonym rather than a synonym is that the two uses answer different questions: one is what a reader took, the other is what a judge concluded. The repair is a name that carries the parallel it actually has, and a check that joins the two carriers so the next collision is refused at the table rather than at the record.
