## Trigger — what happened

A tool refuses to append to a closed register and tries to redirect the producer to the successor. Its successor field admits only an issue reference. The register's actual successor was a directory — the ratified shape for that state class — so the field was left empty deliberately, because writing a plausible issue number would have sent every redirected producer somewhere the register is not. The tool then reported: 'Its closing comment names NO successor... That is a defect in the close.' Every word true of the grammar, and false of that close: the successor existed, was correct, and was simply inexpressible.

## The learning

When a field admits fewer kinds than the world produces, its emptiness stops meaning 'absent' and starts meaning 'absent OR inexpressible' — and any diagnostic phrased over the field reports the first while the second is what happened. The damage is that the message accuses the wrong party: here it named a defect in a close that was careful, and the careful choice (leave it empty rather than write a false pointer) is exactly what triggered the accusation. So a refusal that reports a missing declaration owes a distinction between a field nobody filled and a field that could not hold the answer, and the cheapest way to get it is to widen the grammar to the carrier kinds the design actually ratifies rather than to soften the message. The tell is a correct actor reading an accusation aimed at them for doing the right thing.
