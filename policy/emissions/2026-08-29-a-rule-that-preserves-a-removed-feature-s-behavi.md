## Trigger — what happened

A rule that preserves a removed feature's behaviour so nothing is silently lost — 'a removed entry point's behaviour lives on as a state' — carried a definition that a later ruling had to remove: the preserved record of the retired surface is precisely what re-installed it inside the successor mechanism.

## The learning

A preservation doctrine is a rule about CONTINUITY, and continuity is exactly what makes a superseded definition survive a decision to drop it. When you retire a surface and preserve its behaviour into a new mechanism, you carry across not only the behaviour but the CATEGORY the old surface belonged to — and if a second, unrelated clause later widens that category to solve a different problem, the two clauses collide on the category's NAME and the collision is invisible, because each clause is individually correct and neither mentions the other. The tell is a word doing work in two clauses written at different times for different reasons; the check is to ask, for each clause that uses the word, what it would mean if the word meant only what the other clause needs. And when the collision is resolved, the superseded definition must be deleted from the operative carriers rather than annotated — a note recording what a term used to include is a definition a later reader can act on, which is how the same regeneration happens again. Keep dated version provenance, which a reader needs to tell which version they hold, and delete everything a reader could mistake for current.
