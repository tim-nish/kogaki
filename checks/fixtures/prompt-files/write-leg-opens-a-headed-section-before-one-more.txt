# Write one Leg

## The plan of this paragraph

This block is a plan.
It is not a draft of the paragraph.
Each sentence in it only says what is meant.
None of its wording is kept.
You choose every word of the paragraph.
One thing keeps one name through the whole paragraph.

The claims below are the whole plan of this paragraph.

## The claims this Leg asserts

Each line below is one claim, and every line is a claim of this Leg: none is
subordinate to another. Your prose must assert nothing beyond them: these
lines are the whole of what this Leg may assert.

- the installer copies the kit.

## The Section this Leg sits in

A Section is a grouping of Legs under one heading — one promise to the reader
that the question changes here. This Leg either opens a Section or continues
one, and the line below says which. Where it continues, the heading is already
on the page and you are writing further into it: do not restate the heading's
claim, and do not open a new subject.

- **This Leg OPENS a Section.** Its heading is **"Where the copy lives"**, rendered by the Harness immediately above your prose.
- **Your prose is what the heading promises.** 1 further Leg continues under it, so open the question rather than closing it.

## How the prose reads

**Language.** Write this Leg in English.

**The reader's own world.** The reader has installed a program from a package manager.

**What the register below governs.** The register governs how sentences read.
It changes nothing the plan above says.
Where a register rule and the plan conflict, the plan holds and the rule yields for that sentence.
Do not return a refusal for such a conflict.

**Sentences:** classic register, full clauses, a concrete subject acting,
no unexplained term of art; not fragments, not runs of short sentences, not
triads, not "not X, not Y" runs.

**Paragraphs.** A paragraph's
first sentence names what it refers to in full: no "this", "that", "it",
"these", "those" or a bare definite noun such as "the result" reaching
back into an earlier paragraph. Inside a paragraph, reference is free.

**Referents.** A referent is a case, example, file or person introduced in
the Journey block; it may be named again, by name, in a later paragraph.
Anything else the prose names — including something from the reader's own
world — does not carry across a paragraph boundary and does not exist for
later Legs. A comparison is not a referent:
it explains a term once, and nothing later points back to it.

## Write

Write the prose for this Leg and nothing else. No heading, no leg id, no
label, no commentary about what you are doing.

**The heading is not yours.** One heading is rendered per Section, by the
Harness, from the title the Brief declared — never per Leg and never by you.
Prose that writes its own heading is refused when the Leg is recorded.

**When a step cannot be performed.** If a part of the plan cannot be written from
the material this Packet gives you, or material it should carry is
missing, do not write around it and do not write a sentence telling the
reader so. Return exactly this instead, as the whole of your response:

    refusal: <reason>

This ends the act as a refusal to the Harness, not as content: the Leg is not
recorded, and it is not re-asked, because the input you were given would not
change.
