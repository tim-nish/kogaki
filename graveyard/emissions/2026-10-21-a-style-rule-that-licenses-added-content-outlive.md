## Trigger — what happened

On the 2026-10-20 Leg run, paragraph 3 of the written prose carried a hedge found in no planned point, no claim, no Move segment and no waypoint line of the writer's input. It was traced to a Voice paragraph in the audience's register: 'where a claim's scope is narrower than the sentence that states it, say so in the first person with a hedge'. That paragraph entered on 2026-10-05 as part of a reader-description block, before the paragraph Move and the paragraph plan existed, and moved unedited into the register on 2026-10-14. The hedge also cut against the bound Move, whose own failure test names exactly the effect a hedge after a displacement produces.

## The learning

When a later stage takes over deciding WHAT a paragraph says, re-read every surviving rule from the earlier arrangement and sort each one by what it governs, not by the file it sits in. A rule phrased as style ('say so in the first person with a hedge') that tells the writer to add a qualification is a content rule wearing a style label, and it will fire on a point the plan asserts flatly, adding a sentence the plan never carried and the plan's judge never saw. The test for a register rule is that obeying it changes how the planned content reads and never what the content is; any rule that can add, soften or withdraw a proposition belongs to the planner and its judge, where it is checked, or nowhere.
