## Trigger — what happened

A design record was amended to state a ruling that had just been made. Two of its clauses were written as 'the refusal stays' and 'the file is tracked' — the continuing present — while neither behaviour existed anywhere: the code had no such refusal, and the file was not tracked. The words were reviewed and the reviewer caught them, noting that 'stays' and 'still' assert the continuity of something that never existed, so anyone reading the section before the implementation landed would read the very gap the section was written to name as already closed.

## The learning

A decision record and a description of current state are different documents, and the tense is the only thing separating them when they share a page. Writing a new rule in the continuing present is the natural way to write it — the rule feels true the moment it is decided — and that is exactly what makes the error invisible to its author. The reader who is harmed is the one who arrives in the window between the decision and the work, which is precisely when they most need to know the work is outstanding. Two habits close it. State a newly decided clause as an obligation rather than as a report, and say in the same breath that it is not yet met, naming what will meet it. And when correcting such a clause, leave the old wording quoted rather than deleting it, because a later reader cannot otherwise tell a sentence that was corrected from one that was always right — which is the same distinction, one level up.
