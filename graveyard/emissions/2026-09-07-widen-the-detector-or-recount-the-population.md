## Trigger — what happened

A rule removed every reference matching a pattern. The acceptance grep was widened once when 15 instances hid in an escaped form, and a review then found three more hiding in a form with no punctuation at all. Recounting the population by what a reference IS -- rather than widening the expression a third time -- found a further five that no name-anchored pattern could ever have matched, because they carried no name to anchor on.

## The learning

When a sweep's acceptance is a pattern match and instances keep being found outside it, the instinct is to widen the pattern, and each widening works: it catches the instances just found, and the count returns to zero. But the zero is a reading of the detector, not of the population, and every widening is evidence that the detector was never the thing being measured. The move that ends it is to recount the population by what its members ARE and then ask what the detector sees, because the members that carry none of the punctuation the expression keys on are invisible to every version of it -- so they survive each widening untouched and the search reports success. Two widenings on one sweep is the signal; the second one is where a detector stops being a cheap proxy and starts being the definition.
