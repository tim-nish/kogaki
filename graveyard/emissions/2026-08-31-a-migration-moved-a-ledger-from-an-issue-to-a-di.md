## Trigger — what happened

A migration moved a ledger from an issue to a directory of per-item records, added governing text defining the new carrier, and passed its whole check suite. The routing surface every disposition actually resolves against — a clause elsewhere in the same document — still named the old carrier and still sent readers to it for the carrier's semantics. Both sites were internally correct and neither declared precedence over the other, so a session reading the older one would have kept writing into the carrier the migration existed to eliminate.

## The learning

A migration is complete when the surface READERS RESOLVE AGAINST has moved, not when the new store exists and is documented. Defining the new carrier is the visible, satisfying half and it is the half that leaves the old routing intact, because the definition and the routing are usually different sentences in different sections written for different reasons. The tell is specific and cheap to check: before declaring a carrier migrated, search for every site that NAMES the old carrier and ask of each whether a reader lands there while looking for where to write, rather than only whether the sentence is now false. A site that is merely stale is prose; a site that routes is the migration. And where two sites will coexist for any period, one of them owes an explicit deference to the other, since two correct definitions with no precedence is indistinguishable to a reader from one definition and one mistake.
