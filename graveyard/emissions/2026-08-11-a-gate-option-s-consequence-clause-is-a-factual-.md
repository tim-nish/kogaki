## Trigger — what happened

Framing story 1.53's open question for the owner, I offered 'drop the cite row' with the parenthetical '(cite reachable only via the machine record)'. The owner chose it. The parenthetical was false: SPEC-terrain §12 requires the report to carry the member to served-line map, and its baseline sites that map at the report's end. I had read §14.3, which the question was about, and not §12, which the ANSWER would breach. I had to go back and re-ask.

## The learning

When you offer someone a choice, the part that describes what each option costs is a factual claim, and it is the part least likely to have been checked. The question itself gets scrutiny because it is obviously the thing being asked; the little 'what you lose' note beside each option reads as helpful context and slips through unverified. It is also the part the person actually decides on. The fix is not more care in general: it is to treat every consequence clause as owing the same pointer the question's premises owe, and to check the contracts that govern the OPTION rather than only the ones that govern the QUESTION. Those are different documents, and here they were §12 and §14.3 respectively. A related trap: re-asking after a wrong framing is correct and should not be avoided out of a wish to look decisive, but it must say plainly that the first framing was wrong and why, or the person cannot tell a new consideration from a reversal.
