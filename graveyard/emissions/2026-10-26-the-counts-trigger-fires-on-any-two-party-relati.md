## Trigger — what happened

On the 2026-10-26 one-Leg Proof Sheet (leg-run-2026-10-25T21-45-21-727Z) the counts trigger fired on all three paragraphs and on the Leg Move. The owner reads only two of them as promises: paragraph 3, which asks what decides the variation, says the answer has a counted number of parts and ends on their bare names, and the Leg Move, which ends the same way. Paragraph 1 fired because its last Segment sets the two sides of one pairing against each other, and paragraph 2 because its first Segment divides a rule into two opposed cases; in both the Move goes on to use the counted items itself, so nothing is left open. The owner also ruled that discharge is out of the new Obligation design and that discharged_by and conceded_by are to be removed, with no change made until Obligation has been tested in production.

## The learning

A trigger that reads only whether an operation's wording counts items cannot tell a promise from a description: explanations of a phenomenon routinely set two parties against each other, and the Extractor writes that pair into the operation as a count. What the owner means by an Obligation is a Move that ends while leaving the counted items unexplained. The discriminating form is positional and readable from the Segments alone: the counting or naming Segment is the Move's last, or no later Segment of the same Move operates on the items. Under that reading paragraph 3 and the Leg Move fire and paragraphs 1 and 2 do not. For the rule to be checkable the trigger record must name the Segment it fired on; today it carries only a twelve-word reason. Until such a rule lands, the practical route is for the owner to approve each fired obligation on the sheet before the Move is kept.
