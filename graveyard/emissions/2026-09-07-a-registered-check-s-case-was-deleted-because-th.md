## Trigger — what happened

A registered check's case was deleted because the runtime surface it asserted over was deleted. The suite caught the count immediately: a two-directional floor refused at 95 against a declared 96, and the retirement was recorded in the floor's own note with the case named verbatim. The suite went green. Review then found the record still claiming the retired coverage in a second field — the one a later admission act reads to learn what the member covers — which no arm of the ratchet compares, so the record advertised a property it had stopped checking while every mechanism reported health.

## The learning

A check's registration carries its coverage in two carriers with different enforcement: a COUNT that some gate compares, and a PROSE CLAIM that nothing compares. Retiring a case moves the count, because a mechanism refuses until it does; the claim moves only if someone remembers, and nothing goes red when they do not. So the failure is not that the record is stale — it is that the stale half is the half a later reader trusts, and its staleness is invisible precisely because the enforced half was correctly updated in the same act. The general shape: where a declaration is split across an enforced quantity and an unenforced description of the same fact, updating the enforced half is the moment the unenforced half becomes wrong, and the green suite is what conceals it. Treat the enforced update as the trigger to audit the description, and prefer registrations whose coverage claim is derived from what actually runs rather than restated beside it.
