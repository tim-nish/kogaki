## Trigger — what happened

Deciding where a paragraph's connection to the paragraph before it should live in a plan-then-write pipeline. The first instinct was to fold the connection into the first planned point of the paragraph. The point's own schema said a point is a proposition in the article's subject and never a statement about the discourse, a rule adopted earlier because the writer had narrated stage directions. A connection is a statement about the discourse, so folding it in would either break that rule or flatten the connection into the subject proposition and lose it before the writer saw it.

## The learning

Before adding a new kind of information to an existing field, read that field's contract for the kind of thing it excludes. If the new information is of the excluded kind, give it its own field with its own consumer named, rather than smuggling it into a field whose readers were taught to strip it. A separate field costs one schema key and one rendering line; the smuggled form costs the information itself, silently, at the first reader that enforces the contract. The same test decides whether a closed label set earns its place: name the consumer that reads it, and if the only consumer is a model that needs the prose anyway, send the prose in a structured field and drop the vocabulary.
