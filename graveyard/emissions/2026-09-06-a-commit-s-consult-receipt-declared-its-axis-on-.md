## Trigger — what happened

A commit's consult receipt declared its axis on the line above its query, and the receipt check reported the branch as declaring no axis at all — the two lines were both present and the binding was empty.

## The learning

A continuation key that binds to a neighbouring line has a direction, and writing it on the wrong side produces a record that looks complete and joins to nothing. An axis written above its query is orphaned because the key binds upward to the nearest preceding query, so a reader counting axes sees zero while a human reading the block sees two. The failure is invisible to the author precisely because both lines are there and correctly spelled; only a checker that reports the JOIN rather than the presence can tell them apart. When a grammar gives one key a binding direction, the emitter owes an example in the right order, because the wrong order is not a syntax error and nothing else will surface it.
