## Trigger — what happened

kogaki#1605: a new ParagraphPlan judge axis written as a general rule ('never what a later step produces') was refused on the specimen in only 1 of 5 live draws, because the judge read the specimen's 'that is, ...' clause as the term's meaning, which another criterion asks for

## The learning

When a defect looks like something another criterion asks for, a general wording of the new criterion loses to the existing one. The judge picks the reading that lets both rules pass. Naming the surface form the defect takes (here: an apposition or a 'that is' restatement) moved the refusal rate from 1 in 5 to 5 in 5, and the clean record still passed every time. Bench the first wording before you trust it.
