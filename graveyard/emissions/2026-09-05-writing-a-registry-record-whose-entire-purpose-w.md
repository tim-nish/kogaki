## Trigger — what happened

Writing a registry record whose entire purpose was to fix a misattribution, the sitting inserted a quantitative claim nothing had established: that one issue landed before another 'five issues' earlier. The real distance was 459 issue numbers and about three weeks. Every other sentence in the same edit was sourced — a git sha, a date, a scope-item table — and the licensing issue itself claimed only that one predated the other. The invented number survived a full check suite and reached review, where it was the single finding.

## The learning

A record built to repair an attribution is exactly where an unsourced quantity is least likely to be challenged, because the surrounding sentences are unusually well-evidenced and the reader's trust is calibrated on them. The fabrication was not a guess offered as a guess; it was a specific small integer in the register of a fact, sitting between two real citations. Two properties made it possible and neither is about care: the claim was ORNAMENTAL — dropping it costs the record nothing, so no act depended on it and no check could bind it — and it was PLAUSIBLE-SHAPED, a number of the kind that would be true of something nearby (five PRs had in fact carried the related spec half). The tell is structural rather than psychological: in a passage where every load-bearing claim carries a pointer, any claim WITHOUT one is either droppable or fabricated, and the two look identical. So when writing a provenance record, a quantity with no pointer beside it should be deleted rather than sourced — sourcing invites inventing the source.
