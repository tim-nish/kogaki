## Trigger — what happened

A repository-wide rename of one domain term was applied by case-preserving substitution across the files that matched the word. The substitution could not tell the term from the ordinary English word that spells it, so it renamed a build system's own schema key, a sibling subsystem's unrelated vocabulary whose citations then dangled, the verbatim question recorded in a landed consultation receipt, and the names of rows that had already been retired. The test suite stayed green through all of it, because none of those are things a test looks at.

## The learning

When you rename a term everywhere, the thing you are renaming is a meaning, and the tool you reach for matches a spelling. Decide before you start which occurrences are the term and which merely spell it, and review the ones that merely spell it individually — foreign vocabulary you do not own, ordinary prose, quoted records of what someone said, and the names of things that no longer exist. A green suite is not evidence here: a renamed comment, a renamed citation and a renamed historical name all compile.
