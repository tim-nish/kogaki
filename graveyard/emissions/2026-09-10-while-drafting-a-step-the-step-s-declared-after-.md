## Trigger — what happened

While drafting a step, the step's declared after-state named something its own grounds list did not carry - the reader was to end up knowing a property that none of the three permitted assertions stated. The drafter could satisfy the after-state only by asserting past the grounds, or by deriving it from prose written several steps earlier.

## The learning

When one record field says what a unit may assert and another says what state the unit must leave the reader in, the two can be authored independently and nothing compares them. The gap shows up only at the moment of writing, as a choice between overstepping the permitted inputs and quietly leaning on something outside them - and both look fine afterwards, because the output satisfies each field read on its own. If a pipeline has an inputs field and an outcome field for the same unit, check that the outcome is reachable from the inputs at the point the unit is authored, not at the point it is used.
