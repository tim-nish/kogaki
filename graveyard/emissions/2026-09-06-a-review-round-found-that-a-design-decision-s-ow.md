## Trigger — what happened

A review round found that a design decision's own accepted-cost argument rested on a fact no surface the affected party reads ever stated. The schema note closed a record's key set and accepted the cost as 'a reviewer who wants to annotate has nowhere to put it', resting the arm on the refusal being loud with a one-edit repair — but the reviewer meets that refusal only after writing the record, and the template handed to them enumerated the fields and said nothing about an eighth being refused.

## The learning

When you accept a cost on the ground that the repair is cheap, the fact that makes it cheap is part of the decision and owes a home on the surface the person paying the cost actually reads. Otherwise the argument is sound in the document that reasons about the mechanism and false at the point of use: the loud refusal still fires, but it fires as a surprise rather than as the second half of a bargain someone was told about. The tell is a decision record whose cost paragraph describes an experience no user-facing surface was edited to produce.
