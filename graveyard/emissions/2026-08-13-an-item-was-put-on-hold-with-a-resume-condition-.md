## Trigger — what happened

An item was put on hold with a resume condition stated prominently at the top, and a second precondition — about whether a needed piece of infrastructure existed yet — written further down among the constraints. The top condition was re-checked at every pickup and stayed unmet. The buried one silently became satisfied, and no pickup noticed, because nobody was reading for it.

## The learning

A hold often has more than one thing gating it, and only the one written where the resume check looks gets re-read. The rest sit in the body as background reasoning, indistinguishable in form from explanation that never needed watching — so they go stale in whichever direction, and the record keeps asserting something that stopped being true. The distinguishing question is not how important a statement is but whether it gates WHEN the work may begin: anything that does belongs beside the resume condition, and anything that does not is genuinely just context. Worth noticing that the failure is invisible from either end. A pickup reading the top condition correctly concludes the hold stands; a reader of the body meets a claim about infrastructure that is simply false, with nothing marking it as an old measurement rather than a current one. Neither is wrong about what they read. The cheap habit is, when writing a hold, to ask of each stated precondition whether a later reader would need to re-check it, and to move those few into the same place the resume check already looks.
