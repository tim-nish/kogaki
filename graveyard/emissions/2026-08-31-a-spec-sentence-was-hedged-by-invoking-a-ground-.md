## Trigger — what happened

A spec sentence was hedged by invoking a ground stated elsewhere in the same document, and the ground was attributed to the section the sentence itself sits inside. That section never states it; the phrase's only prior use was in a different section, which happened to reach it about the same subject. The attribution was wrong while the ground was right, and the sentence doing this was the repair for a finding about claims attributed to sources that do not carry them.

## The learning

When a writer reaches for an established ground to justify a new sentence, the cheapest candidate is the section already open in front of them, and physical proximity is mistaken for the relation that matters, which is whether that section states the thing. The error is unusually stable because the ground is genuinely present in the document, so every reading that checks WHETHER THE DOCUMENT SUPPORTS THE CLAIM returns yes and the attribution is never the thing under test. It also survives self-review specifically, because the author reads the citation as a gesture at a shared idea rather than as a pointer that has a truth value. The check that catches it is to search the document for the invoked phrase and confirm the section named is the section returned, and the durable repair is to name the clause by what it says rather than by where it sits.
