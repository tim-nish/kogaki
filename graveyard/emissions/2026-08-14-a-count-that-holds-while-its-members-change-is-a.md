## Trigger — what happened

A report measured a problem population as nine items. A day later the same measurement returned nine again, and the natural reading was that nothing had changed and the number could be carried forward. Listing the members showed two had left and two new ones had arrived. The count was identical and the situation it described was not: the population was refilling at the rate it drained.

## The learning

Re-measure a population by listing its members, not by taking its size, whenever the question is whether the problem is getting better. A stable count is ambiguous between nothing happening and an inflow matching an outflow, and those call for opposite responses: the first can be waited out, the second cannot, because waiting is exactly the strategy that fails against a steady state. The two are indistinguishable from the number alone, and the comparison that separates them costs one extra listing. Report the composition change alongside the count when you have it, because a reader given only the stable number will reach for the wait-and-see reading by default, and that is the wrong one precisely in the case that matters.
