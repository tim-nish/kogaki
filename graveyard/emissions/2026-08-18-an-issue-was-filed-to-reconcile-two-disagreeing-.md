## Trigger — what happened

An issue was filed to reconcile two disagreeing records — a check's summary line and a descriptive note beside it, which named a piece of evidence the summary omitted. The issue treated the summary as the supported artifact and the note as the error. Settling it against the original history found the note's claim was REAL but superseded, and that the attribution BOTH artifacts carried was unsupported by the source either of them derived from.

## The learning

When two records disagree and you set out to decide which wins, the reflex is to pick the one with better standing — the generated one over the hand-written one, the newer over the older, the one the machine prints over the one a person typed. Check the SOURCE before you rank them, because the artifact you are about to promote can be wrong in the same way, and promoting it launders an unsupported claim into a settled one.

Here the original record attributed four pieces of evidence JOINTLY to two sources. Both later artifacts split that set between the two, differently, and neither split was in the original. The issue's own framing had already picked a winner, so the correction it licensed would have fixed one artifact and left the identical defect standing in the other — with the fix as evidence that the surviving one was right.

The second half is about what a removed record leaves behind. One artifact named evidence the file no longer carries, because a later re-cut removed the assertion that evidence broke. That is SUPERSEDED, not invented, and the two read identically once the phrase is deleted — so a reader who later notices the gap re-opens it as a missing item rather than a settled one. Deleting the phrase is not enough: re-home its disposition, or the removal is temporary.

Practical form: before adjudicating between records, read the earliest one that either derives from, and state which of them the source actually supports — including the possibility that it supports neither.
