## Trigger — what happened

On the 2026-10-19 attack-defense Leg run every paragraph's first sentence was longer and harder than the original's. Paragraph 3's original connective was one word (therefore); its plan join said the second half was still owed and this paragraph carried the settled half one step further, and the written sentence began 'American society repays one further step before its counterpart is set beside it, because'. Paragraph 5, whose join said the same kind of thing, read fine.

## The learning

A connection handed to the writer as a prose sentence about the content is realised as content, however firmly the instruction says it is a connection only. The surplus in a paragraph's first sentence is exactly the join's content that the paragraph's own first point does not already carry: paragraph 5's first point was itself a drawing-together, so the join cost three words; paragraphs 3 and 4's first points were bare facts, so the join cost a clause. Two rules amplified it. The judge's requirement that the join take up what the previous Move's after-state leaves open produced a false connection where the original defers (paragraph 3 continues the first case while the after-state wants the second), and the planner wrapped the falsehood in procedural wording the writer then narrated. The ban on naming noun phrases from the points made the planner describe the content in circumlocutions ('the settled half', 'the waiting side'), and the writer rendered the circumlocutions. The Segment relation has no such problem because it is consumed at planning and never reaches the writer. A connection meant to cost zero or one word must be carried as something that cannot be paraphrased, or kept out of the writer's input altogether.
