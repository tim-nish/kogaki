## Trigger — what happened

A ruling reduced one gate's displayed material to its option labels. Three sections over, a separate rule justified skipping a per-item approval question on the ground that 'the flags already expose every bridge's reasoning at the one gate that exists'. That ground was true when written and false the moment the display was removed. Nothing in either document referred to the other, so the amendment passed every check, and the approval step it broke would have gone on reading as satisfied.

## The learning

When a rule justifies itself by pointing at something another part of the system displays, computes or guarantees, that justification is a dependency the writing does not record anywhere a later editor will look. Removing the thing it points at leaves the rule textually untouched and substantively hollow: an approval whose disclosure surface is gone still reads as post-hoc approval, and nothing goes red, because a ground is prose and no test asserts one. The cheap habit that catches it is to ask, when removing or shrinking any surface, who was relying on it being there - and to search for the removed thing by name rather than trusting that a dependent would have cited you. What is owed on finding one is a written statement that the ground has failed, at the clause it supports, even when the repair is a separate decision you are not entitled to make; leaving it silent is the failure, not leaving it unrepaired.
