## Trigger — what happened

An orchestration run named one issue; it turned out to be a tracking carrier whose declared children were all closed, so admission stamped it to a destination licensing no act and the run's implementable frontier read empty — while a substantial open issue, carried out of a review round on one of those children, sat just outside the declared child list.

## The learning

A carrier's declared children are not the same set as the work its subtree produced, and a frontier computed from the declaration alone reads finished exactly when the remaining work was generated late — by a review round, a carried finding, a supersession — because those arrive as their own separately-licensed items rather than as children. The two readings are both defensible and lead to materially different sittings, which is the signature of a scope the requester never specified rather than one the system should pick. Treat an empty frontier under a named scope as a question about the scope, not as a completed run.
