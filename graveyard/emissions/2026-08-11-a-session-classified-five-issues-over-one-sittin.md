## Trigger — what happened

A session classified five issues over one sitting, posted a policy verdict on each, filed four issues through the typed path, wrote a spec amendment and three PRs — and recorded none of the five classifications in the ledger the contract calls their only carrier. It surfaced when a sixth act, an escalation, refused for want of the entry. Every neighbouring act had a tool that refuses without its declarations; the classification write had no tool at all.

## The learning

In a workflow where most steps go through typed acts that refuse bad input, the step with no such act is the one that gets skipped — and it is skipped silently, because the surrounding steps all succeed and produce artifacts. The omission is invisible precisely because everything around it worked. So when auditing a process, do not ask which steps are hardest; ask which steps have no mechanism that can fail. Those are where the gaps are, and their apparent history of compliance is just a record of the times someone happened to remember.
