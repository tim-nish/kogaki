## Trigger — what happened

A conformance block was written and its subject axis consulted and grounded — what the act was about. A review round found the conduct axis ungrounded: nothing had asked how the act was conducted. The second query looked like a formality owed to a rule. It returned a line about checkers whose fallback message is phrased as a positive finding, and applying it exposed two live defects in the very check that had just been written: two assertions extracted a figure by regex and would have reported an internal parse miss as a finding against the code under test.

## The learning

When you consult about a piece of work, the obvious question is what the work is about, and answering it well feels like having consulted. But how the work is conducted is a separate question with separate answers, and the first question's answer cannot contain the second's — a check can be about exactly the right property and still accuse the wrong party when its own machinery slips. The tell that the second question is missing is that nothing in the record says anything about manner: every citation is about subject matter. Ask it as a real question rather than to satisfy the rule, because the payoff is not the receipt; twice now the manner question has returned something that changed the code, and a question asked to discharge an obligation gets answered at the depth of the obligation rather than the depth of the work.
