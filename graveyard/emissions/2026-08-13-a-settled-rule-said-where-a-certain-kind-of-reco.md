## Trigger — what happened

A settled rule said where a certain kind of record must be kept, and named two places it must never be kept. The place it pointed to had never been created. Nothing had gone wrong yet, because nobody had produced a record of that kind — but the moment someone did, the only places to put it were the two the rule forbade. The rule had also been deliberately exempted from any automated checking, so nothing would have objected.

## The learning

A rule that forbids two destinations and names a third only works if the third one exists. Until it does, the rule is a prohibition rather than a constraint, and prohibitions get worked around at exactly the moment they bind — because the person hitting it has real work to place and no legal place to put it, so they widen one of the forbidden options and the widening looks reasonable in isolation. The gap is invisible while nobody is producing the thing, which is also the only period when it is cheap to close. Two practical readings follow. When a rule names a destination, treat the destination's existence as part of the rule rather than as follow-up work, and check for it at the moment the rule is written. And when deciding whether an unbuilt thing is worth building now, the ordinary advice to wait until real use surfaces the need does not apply to this shape: waiting for use means waiting for the exact moment the workaround becomes attractive. What waiting is right for is unbuilt work that no rule already depends on.
